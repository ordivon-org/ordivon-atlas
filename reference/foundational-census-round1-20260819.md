## Round-1 findings

- **FCR1-1 [ESTABLISHED_IN_SCOPE]** — The external systems classify different things: policy priorities, R&D statistics, education fields and library holdings. Their disagreement is expected and informative.
- **FCR1-2 [SUPPORTED_IN_SCOPE]** — Several spaces recur strongly across views: mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, engineering/technology, earth/geoscience, medicine/health and agriculture; recurrence does not by itself determine foundational root status.
- **FCR1-3 [SUPPORTED_IN_SCOPE]** — Formal/computational spaces are fragmented by institutional views: mathematics/statistics, computer-information sciences, logic, information and systems appear at different levels, supporting a multi-node formal/abstract family rather than one Computer Science bucket.
- **FCR1-4 [SUPPORTED_IN_SCOPE]** — Earth/ocean/atmosphere is too large to remain an incidental child of generic physics or geography; source views repeatedly expose geology/geophysics/meteorology/physical geography or separate geography classes.
- **FCR1-5 [SUPPORTED_IN_SCOPE]** — Mind/language domains are classification-pressure zones: psychology, linguistics, cognitive science and neuroscience cut across natural, medical, social, humanities and library views.
- **FCR1-6 [PROVISIONAL_SUPPORTED]** — Methodological foundations such as measurement/metrology can be central to knowledge production while being underrepresented as academic root disciplines; the Atlas census therefore needs a methodological-axis view in addition to discipline views.

No candidate above is admitted as a final canonical root merely because several source systems contain it. The census remains open.
