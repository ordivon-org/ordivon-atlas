# Next Conversation Handoff — Agenda Power / Option-Space Governance

## Standing

`OPEN_PROBLEM / SELECTED_CONVERSATION_FRONTIER / NOT_A_ROADMAP`

## Immediate question

`OptionGeneration != OptionSelection`.

A downstream settlement procedure may be fair relative to the candidates it receives while an upstream actor, evaluator, representation, retrieval system, institution, or worldview has already shaped which candidates can appear.

The next inquiry should therefore ask:

> When a system eventually chooses one real trajectory from several admissible alternatives, who or what had causal power over the **formation of the option space itself** before selection began?

## Why this follows from the closed conversation

The parent conversation progressively de-collapsed:

`Evidence -> Standing -> Admission -> Settlement -> Commitment -> Effect -> Propagation`.

It then found that final settlement need not imply substantive resolution. That leaves an earlier boundary unresolved: the candidate set presented to the procedure may itself already encode exclusions, framing, representation limits, authority filters, or search adjacency.

## First hostile questions

Begin broadly; do not assume one theory already owns the problem.

- Can a fair selector still be structurally biased because the option generator omitted a load-bearing alternative?
- What is the difference between option absence, option inadmissibility, option unrepresentability, and option suppression?
- Who has authority to introduce an option, and is proposal access distinct from selection power?
- Can search/retrieval architecture act as shadow agenda authority without explicit governance standing?
- How do minority/beneficiary options survive if the dominant representation cannot naturally generate them?
- How do we test option-space blindness without pretending to enumerate all ungenerated alternatives?
- When does broadening the option space improve intelligence, and when does it create noise, unsafe actions, or governance explosion?
- How should historical rejected/superseded options remain recoverable without automatically re-entering the active option set?
