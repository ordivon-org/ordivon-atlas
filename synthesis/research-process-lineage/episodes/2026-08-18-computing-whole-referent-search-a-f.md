## Question

What is the actual semantic referent of the provisional Ordivon “Computing / Programming Systems” space?

Is it:

- one coherent owner;
- several independent semantic owners;
- or an invalid/overly broad cut whose contents belong elsewhere?

The search explicitly forbade assuming a canonical project name, a Computing primitive, a Foundation number, or even `ProjectCount=1` before whole-referent coverage.

## Initial position

“Computing / Programming Systems” was treated as a provisional umbrella broad enough to contain:

- computational descriptions/models and their meaning;
- semantics-preserving transformation;
- computability/solvability;
- algorithms, strategies and methods;
- resource/complexity/lower-bound questions;
- programming systems and OS-like mechanisms;
- classical, probabilistic, interactive, distributed, quantum, continuous/analog, learning and Agent-era cases.

The key risk was **owner inflation by familiar discipline label**: taking the human category “Computing” and minting it directly as one Ordivon truth owner.

## Search strategy

The A–F campaign progressively widened and attacked the umbrella rather than naming a project early.

The detailed per-round conversational transcript is not reconstructed here. The durable closeout establishes that by Search F the campaign had tested candidate responsibility across multiple semantic regimes and was explicitly running a project-admission / separate whole-domain-coverage tournament.

The load-bearing comparison became:

- do the candidate subspaces share one truth condition and reopen boundary?
- can one change/falsifier affect one family without changing the other?
- do they have different consumers and independent closure cadence?
- can apparent additional subdomains be reduced to typed subfamilies or external owners?

## Candidate S

A coherent responsibility family survived around:

**computational description/model semantics and semantics-preserving transformation**.

Hostile coverage included functional, imperative, axiomatic/algebraic, logic/rewrite/query, process/reactive/dataflow, nondeterministic/probabilistic, quantum, continuous/analog, population and Agent-generated/self-modifying regimes.

The semantic carrier/BehaviorObject varied, but the owner question remained approximately:

> What behavior/meaning does this admitted computational description or model have, what observations/equivalences/refinements hold, and what transformations preserve the required behavior?

CompCert-style semantic preservation, Kozen-style probabilistic semantics, process/reactive semantics, quantum/continuous cases and Agent-generated artifacts all pressured the candidate without forcing separate owners.

## Candidate T

A second coherent responsibility family survived around:

**computational problem possibility, methods/strategies and limits**.

Coverage included classical computability, deterministic/randomized algorithms, approximation, online/competitive methods, communication/query/resource models, quantum algorithms/complexity, distributed/population models, learning/sample complexity and unconventional models.

The changing regimes still preserved a common Problem–Model–Method–Success/Limit structure.

Examples such as population-protocol computability classes, Valiant-style learnability and online strategy analysis broadened “method” beyond a closed terminating algorithm.

## Decisive falsifier against one broad owner

The single-Computing-owner hypothesis lost because S and T showed:

- independent truth conditions;
- independent falsifiers/reopen conditions;
- weak change coupling;
- different principal consumers;
- independent closure cadence.

An incorrect semantics/equivalence/transformation claim can falsify S without changing T's solvability/complexity result. A new algorithm/lower bound/computability result can alter T without changing the meaning of the computational descriptions in S.

Therefore the broad umbrella did not carry one sufficiently strong semantic responsibility.

## Rival splits also tested and rejected

The search did **not** simply split everything into projects.

Rejected immediate third-owner candidates included:

- Resource / Complexity — retained inside T as a projection over the same problem/model/method space;
- Process / Interaction Semantics — retained as typed S semantics;
- Probabilistic / Quantum / Unconventional semantics — retained as typed S subfamilies where appropriate;
- Programming Systems / OS — current foundational truths decompose across S + T + Runtime rather than requiring an independent owner;
- `ComputationalRole` / `ComputationalModel` — retained as a shared admission/index interface, not a third owner.

Security, World, Runtime, Network, Harness and other source-domain truths remained outside the umbrella except for explicitly computational projections.

## Revision / result

Search F admitted:

`ProjectCount = 2` at semantic owner-line level.

Provisional navigation labels:

- S = `Computational Semantics & Transformation`;
- T = `Computational Possibility / Methods / Limits`.

But the campaign deliberately left:

- `CanonicalNameS = UNKNOWN`;
- `CanonicalNameT = UNKNOWN`;
- `Foundation0S = NOT_ADMITTED`;
- `Foundation0T = NOT_ADMITTED`.

This mattered: owner decomposition was established **without pretending that project identity or primitive Foundations had already been discovered**.

## Surviving insight

The most important process result is not merely “there are two projects.” It is the owner-discovery method:

> Test truth responsibility, falsifiers, change coupling, consumers and closure cadence before turning a familiar disciplinary umbrella into one semantic owner.

This later allowed S and T to evolve independently into Semantics of Computational Descriptions and Computational Possibility.
